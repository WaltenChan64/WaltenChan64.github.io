Overview

About the project

A lighthearted 3D stealth puzzle game where you play as Applewood — a living puff of smoke with a knack for sneaky strategy. Bamboozle bakers and outwit the janitor as you hunt down the ultimate, elusive burnt pie.

I was the Usability Lead on a 40+ person team in USC's Advanced Game Project (AGP) program, responsible for designing and running the playtest process throughout development. I also contributed programming work on top of my primary role.

Although my primary role was Usability Lead, I also contributed programming work to improve player comprehension and game feel directly. This included enemy vision cones — a visual indicator system showing players exactly what enemies can see, helping them make informed stealth decisions.

I also implemented dynamic animation splines, which smoothly transitioned players into cutscenes, regardless of where they were in the level.

Primary Role — Usability Lead

Running the playtest process

I designed and conducted weekly playtests throughout development, creating structured test sessions and systematically documenting what I found. Over the course of the project I identified 70+ usability issues and 80+ bugs — working with the full team to track and address them.

Many of the issues involved player comprehension — places where the game wasn't communicating clearly enough what the player was supposed to do, or why something wasn't working. Bridging that gap between what the developers intended and what players actually experienced was some of the most valuable work on the project.

💡 Running a structured playtest process on a 40+ person team meant the whole project stayed grounded in how real players actually experienced the game — not just how we imagined they would.
